# Agents recording cues

Draft prompts for Josh and Mary, 2026-10-02. These are optional conversation scaffolding, not invented dialogue or a requirement to make the recorded episode 60 minutes.

## Central question

**What would we actually trust an AI agent to do for us?**

Proposed recording structure: six beats, approximately 40-50 minutes if the conversation earns it. Times are pacing guides, not an adopted episode runtime. Keep one running example: planning an event with a venue comparison, a budget and draft volunteer communication. Describe it as hypothetical unless an actual run has been completed.

| Beat | Suggested time | What to hit | Conversation prompt / transition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. What would we hand off? | 3-5 minutes | Each chooses one actual annoying task and one boundary. Establish the appeal of delegation. | “What would you most want this thing to take off your plate? Where would you want it to stop?” Then: “What does it need to do to deserve that trust?” |
| 2. What makes it an agent? | 5-7 minutes | Answering versus carrying out work; predefined automation versus choosing next steps. Acknowledge definitions vary. | Compare “tell me how to plan an event” with “research options and prepare the comparison.” Then: “How does it actually do that?” |
| 3. How does it work? | 8-10 minutes | Goal, context, tools and permissions; observe, choose, act, check, adjust. Connect to E002. | Walk through a missing Saturday venue price and the next action. Each host explains a piece and challenges an assumption. Then: “Where is that flexibility worth using?” |
| 4. Where would it help us? | 8-10 minutes | Research, coding or operating examples; explain a task, its output and what the person retains. Use one real story or completed host experiment. | Discuss Project Vend's first phase and later improvement, with dates and small-experiment limits. Both assess what changed their expectations. Then: “What would we need to check before relying on it?” |
| 5. How much would we trust it? | 8-10 minutes | False completion, compounding errors, outside instructions/prompt injection, permissions, review effort. Tie each to the running task. | Each chooses among research, drafting, sending and committing money, and explains why. Ask whether verification leaves a net benefit. Then: “What should someone try first?” |
| 6. What should we try next? | 4-6 minutes | Each picks a task and a supervision boundary. Briefly consider what better capability would change. End with a usable delegation recipe. | Give a goal, constraints, allowed actions and evidence of completion. Revisit the opening choices: did either boundary move? |

## Keep the conversation natural

- Keep the six questions visible. Finish each beat with one clear answer, then use the bridge when the conversation naturally slows.
- Alternate who introduces a topic and who evaluates it; both explain, react and challenge. Choose handoffs based on what each actually knows or has tried.
- Preserve a worthwhile tangent or laugh. When a tangent raises a new useful concept, connect it to the central question; park repeated explanations or an unrelated product tour.
- Explain each technical term through the running task. History, MCP, training mechanics and benchmarks are optional depth, not a vocabulary checklist.
- An actual prepared result can strengthen beat four, but this structure works without a live demo. Make the inputs and outcome understandable through audio.
- If time is tight, shorten extra examples and future speculation; retain the definition, action loop, trust decision and practical takeaway.

## Evidence available if needed

- Project Vend: first phase report June 2025; improved second phase report December 2025, with manipulation weaknesses remaining. Do not tell only the early failure story.
- If using the 95%-per-step illustration, ten independent necessary steps produce about 60% overall success. It is invented arithmetic, not a measured AI failure rate.
- If using the 19% productivity slowdown, attach the experienced-maintainer population, familiar repositories and early-2025 tools. Do not claim a universal current result.
- Benchmark time horizon measures human task difficulty at a stated success level, not AI runtime. Keep this only if a benchmark claim arises.

## Optional demonstration instruction

“Using only public sources, prepare three plausible location options for our hypothetical Saturday morning community event. Use the date, budget, attendance, travel limit, accessibility needs and weather-backup requirements we supply. Create a local comparison with sources for material claims. Separate verified details from unknowns and recommend which option to investigate first. Do not contact anyone, reserve anything or send messages. Stop when the comparison is saved and checked, or explain which requirement could not be satisfied.”

Fill in those constraints before use. Record tool/account/version/date, input, elapsed time, host intervention, review effort and actual result. No demonstration was performed as part of this primer.

## Say accurately

- “This is a useful working definition of agent.”
- “It created a draft” only after checking that draft; “it booked” requires an actual reservation.
- “This study measured this population with these tools at this time.”
- “The company's August 2026 index reports human-supervised work; it does not report fully autonomous AI R&D.”
- “Here is our experience from this task,” once the hosts actually have it.
