Level 1 of 6 / 6 words

Say hi

What AI is, what to call things, and the one habit that fixes most bad answers.

Generative AI

AI that makes new things, like writing, images, music or video.

Think of it like The difference between a spam filter and a ghostwriter.

Not on the show yet

Prompt

Whatever you type or say to the AI. The best ones read like a text to a smart friend who knows nothing about your life.

Think of it like Ordering "food, please" vs. "the salmon, sauce on the side."

Watch us explain it: ep 004 at 16:23

LLM Large language model

The engine inside ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini. It read a huge chunk of the internet and learned to guess the next word.

Think of it like Autocomplete that read the whole library.

Watch us explain it: ep 002 at 06:20

Model vs. app Who's who

OpenAI is the company. GPT-6 Astra is one of its models. ChatGPT is the app you open.

Think of it like Toyota, the engine and the Prius.

Watch us explain it: ep 002 at 08:49

Hallucination

When AI makes something up with a straight face, like a quote nobody said or a study that doesn't exist.

Think of it like The friend who'd rather invent an answer than say "I don't know."

Watch us explain it: ep 003 at 22:09

Multimodal

AI that understands more than text. Send it a photo or a voice memo.

Think of it like Snap a photo of your fridge and ask what's for dinner.

Watch us explain it: ep 002 at 08:29

Try it / about 10 minutes

  1. Ask for a birthday message twice. First just "write a birthday message." Then write it like a text to a smart friend: who it's for, how you know them, one inside joke, how long. Compare the two.
  2. Ask it for a fact with a source. Click the source. Does it exist and say what the AI claimed?
  3. Snap a photo of your fridge and ask what you can make for dinner.

Check yourself

Say the answer out loud, like you're explaining it to a friend.

  1. In one sentence, what's the difference between a model and an app?
  2. Why does a better prompt usually beat a better model?
  3. Name one time you'd double check an AI answer before using it.

Level 2 of 6 / 6 words

Make it yours

Stop re-explaining yourself. Set it up once so every chat starts smarter.

Memory

The app remembering things about you between chats, like your job or that you're vegetarian. You can edit it in settings.

Think of it like A barista who knows your usual.

Not on the show yet

Custom instructions

Notes the AI reads before every chat, like "keep it short" or "I run a nonprofit."

Think of it like The note on the fridge for the babysitter.

Watch us explain it: ep 004 at 09:09

Projects

A folder for one ongoing thing, with its own chats, files and instructions, so you never re-explain the backstory.

Think of it like Giving your AI its own desk.

Watch us explain it: ep 001 at 01:43

Model sizes

Most AI companies sell a few sizes. Bigger is smarter, smaller is faster and cheaper. OpenAI's are Astra, Sol and Luna.

Think of it like Coffee sizes. Not every question needs a venti.

Not on the show yet

Reasoning model Thinking mode

A model that thinks through a problem before answering. Slower, but much better at math and anything with moving parts.

Think of it like Showing your work instead of blurting out the first number.

Watch us explain it: ep 004 at 13:36

Deep research

Ask a big question, go make coffee, come back to a report with sources. The AI runs dozens of searches for you.

Think of it like An intern who does the library run for you.

Not on the show yet

Try it / about 10 minutes

  1. Write three lines of custom instructions: who you are, how you like answers, and one thing to never do.
  2. Start a project for one ongoing thing in your life, like a trip, a job search or a class. Add one file.
  3. Give the same planning question to the regular mode and to the thinking mode. Which answer would you actually use?

Check yourself

Say the answer out loud, like you're explaining it to a friend.

  1. What's the difference between memory and custom instructions?
  2. When is a small, fast model the right pick?
  3. What kind of question is worth a deep research report?

Level 3 of 6 / 5 words

Spot the traps

The ways AI quietly lets you down, and the easy habits that protect you.

Knowledge cutoff

The date a model's built-in knowledge stops. Ask about anything newer and it's guessing, unless it can search the web.

Think of it like A smart friend back from six months off the grid.

Not on the show yet

AI slop

The bland, over-polished writing AI churns out when nobody edits it. "I hope this finds you well," forever.

Think of it like Elevator music, but for words.

Watch us explain it: ep 001 at 15:42

Sycophancy

When AI tells you what you want to hear, like calling your so-so business plan brilliant.

Think of it like A yes-man with a big vocabulary.

Watch us explain it: ep 004 at 23:43

Deepfake

Fake but real-looking video, audio or photos of a real person. Voice clones make scam calls sound like family.

Think of it like Photoshop, if Photoshop could call your grandma.

Not on the show yet

Prompt injection

Sneaky instructions hidden in a webpage or email that trick AI into doing something you didn't ask for.

Think of it like A note slipped into the to-do pile: "also, wire $500 to this account."

Not on the show yet

Try it / about 10 minutes

  1. Share an idea you like and ask: "What would a smart skeptic say? Be blunt." Compare that to its first reaction.
  2. Ask about something from this week with web search off, then on. Notice the difference.
  3. Pick a family safe word you'd ask for if "you" ever called asking for money.

Check yourself

Say the answer out loud, like you're explaining it to a friend.

  1. How can you tell a model is past its knowledge cutoff?
  2. What are two signs a piece of writing is AI slop?
  3. Why is prompt injection a bigger deal once AI can take actions for you?

Level 4 of 6 / 9 words

Under the hood

Enough of how it works to follow the news and ask good questions.

Training

How a model learns. It reads a mountain of text over and over until the patterns stick.

Think of it like Years of reading before the first day on the job.

Watch us explain it: ep 002 at 06:20

Token

The bite-size chunks AI reads and writes in, about three quarters of a word each. Prices and limits are counted in them.

Think of it like Gas in the car. Every word in and out burns a little.

Watch us explain it: ep 002 at 13:26

Context window

How much the AI can hold in its head at once, including your messages, its replies and your files. In long chats, the early stuff starts to slip.

Think of it like A whiteboard. Fill it up and something gets erased.

Not on the show yet

Open vs. closed models

Open models, like DeepSeek and Qwen, can be downloaded and run by anyone. Closed ones, like the models in ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, only live behind the company's app.

Think of it like A recipe you can cook at home vs. a dish you can only order at the restaurant.

Watch us explain it: ep 002 at 06:20

Benchmark

A standardized test for AI. Labs love bragging about scores, and the tests keep getting harder because models keep acing them.

Think of it like The SAT, for robots.

Not on the show yet

GPU Graphics processing unit

The chips that do AI's heavy math. Nvidia makes most of them, and everyone wants more.

Think of it like Gaming graphics cards that got drafted into a much bigger job.

Not on the show yet

Data center

Warehouses full of those chips, running around the clock. Their power and water use is a big part of the AI debate.

Think of it like The kitchen behind every AI answer.

Not on the show yet

RAG Retrieval-augmented generation

AI that checks your documents before answering, so it can cite sources instead of going from memory.

Think of it like An open-book test instead of a closed-book one.

Not on the show yet

Fine-tuning

Extra training on a focused set of examples so a model gets great at one job, like writing in your company's voice.

Think of it like A residency after med school.

Not on the show yet

Try it / about 10 minutes

  1. Search "tokenizer" and paste in a sentence. Watch how it gets chopped into tokens.
  2. Upload a long PDF and ask three questions, each with the page number of the answer. Check one.
  3. In a very long chat, ask what you said in your first message. Did it slip?

Check yourself

Say the answer out loud, like you're explaining it to a friend.

  1. Why do long chats sometimes get worse?
  2. RAG or fine-tuning: which one would you use to answer questions about your company handbook, and why?
  3. Why do AI companies care so much about GPUs and data centers?

The whole thing on one page

Learn, look, think, act

Almost every AI headline is about one of these four steps. Figure out which one, and the news gets a lot less confusing.

Learn

Training. The model reads a mountain of text and the patterns stick. Happens once, months before you ever open the app.

Training, GPUs, data centers, fine-tuning
Look

Pulling in fresh info at the moment you ask: a web search, your files, your inbox.

RAG, deep research, MCP
Think

Working out the answer, one token at a time, inside the context window.

Tokens, context window, reasoning models
Act

Doing something in the world: sending, booking, clicking, writing code.

Agents, skills, computer use

Level 5 of 6 / 5 words

AI that does things

The jump from answering questions to getting work done for you.

Agent

AI you give a goal instead of a question. It picks the steps, uses tools like search or your calendar, and keeps going until it's done.

Think of it like Asking for directions vs. handing someone your car keys.

Watch all of ep 003

Skills

Saved instructions for specific jobs, like your brand rules or how you write a grant. The AI grabs the right one when it needs it.

Think of it like A binder of how-tos a new hire actually reads.

Watch us explain it: ep 001 at 08:12

MCP Model Context Protocol

A standard plug that connects AI to your other apps, like Gmail or Google Drive, so it can get things done there.

Think of it like USB for AI. One plug shape, lots of devices.

Watch us explain it: ep 001 at 05:59

Computer use

AI using a computer the way you do, looking at the screen, clicking and typing.

Think of it like Handing someone your laptop and watching over their shoulder.

Not on the show yet

Vibe coding

Building an app by describing it in plain English and letting AI write all the code.

Think of it like Telling a builder what house you want and never looking at the blueprints.

Watch us explain it: ep 004 at 23:52

Try it / about 10 minutes

  1. Vibe code something tiny: "Make me a tip calculator for splitting dinner with friends." Use it once for real.
  2. Connect one app you already use, like your calendar or Drive, and ask it to find something for you.
  3. Give an agent a job with a clear finish line, like "find three Saturday hikes under an hour away and put them in a table."

Check yourself

Say the answer out loud, like you're explaining it to a friend.

  1. What makes something an agent and not just a chatbot?
  2. What's one task you'd hand to an agent, and one you wouldn't yet?
  3. What would you want to approve before AI clicks buy or send for you?

Level 6 of 6 / 4 words

The big questions

The debates everyone is having. You don't need answers, just your own take.

Scaling laws

The pattern that models get smarter as they get bigger and train on more data with more chips. Whether it keeps holding is the trillion-dollar question.

Think of it like Adding horsepower to a car. Works great, until it doesn't.

Not on the show yet

Alignment

Getting AI to do what people actually want, without cutting corners or causing harm. It matters more as models get smarter.

Think of it like Raising a kid who'll end up smarter than you.

Not on the show yet

AGI Artificial general intelligence

AI that can do pretty much any thinking job a person can. Nobody agrees on the definition or when it arrives.

Think of it like A finish line everyone's racing toward, but each team drew it somewhere else.

Not on the show yet

Superintelligence

AI that beats the best humans at nearly everything. The step after AGI.

Think of it like The student who passes the teacher and keeps going.

Not on the show yet

Try it / about 10 minutes

  1. Pick one big question below. Write your own opinion in three sentences before you ask AI anything.
  2. Then ask AI for the strongest argument against you. Did it change your mind or sharpen it?
  3. Bring it up at dinner. See where people land.

Check yourself

Say the answer out loud, like you're explaining it to a friend.

  1. If AGI arrived tomorrow, how would you know?
  2. Who should decide what AI is allowed to do?
  3. What's one thing you'd never hand to AI, no matter how good it gets?
