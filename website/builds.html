This website

You are looking at it. We described what we wanted and Claude wrote every line of code

How we built it

  1. We said what we wanted in plain words and pasted screenshots of sites we love
  2. Claude built a few versions. We picked one, the same way you would pick a paint color

Your turn. Copy this into any AI app

Build me a simple one-page website for [what it is for]. Here are two sites I like the look of: [links or screenshots]. Show me three different designs first so I can pick one, then build the one I choose.

AI planned our wedding

We skipped the wedding planner. Mary gave Josh a trial period: if it got stressful, we would hire one. We never did

How we built it

  1. Before building anything, we had AI interview us: which events we were hosting, which small touches we cared about and what we would skip
  2. Then it built the tracker. Every task got a category, a due date, an owner, a status and a priority, so Mary could filter by her name and see her list
  3. After vendor calls, AI read our notes and told us which rows to add, change or close. Handy, because our venue forgot what we picked at the tasting

A website for our wedding weekend

One page with everything guests needed that weekend, so they didn't have to text us questions

How we built it

  1. We showed Claude our first wedding website so the colors and fonts matched
  2. We connected it to our guest list sheet, so who's coming stayed current as RSVPs changed
  3. We added what guests ask about: add-to-calendar buttons, map links, an FAQ with one Easter egg and a button that drops photos into a shared Google Drive folder

Your turn. Copy this into any AI app

Build a one-page website for guests at our wedding weekend in [place]. Include the schedule with add-to-calendar buttons and map links, shuttle times, a short FAQ (dress code, parking, kids), a few of our favorite local spots and a button to upload photos to a shared folder. Match the colors and fonts of our wedding site: [link]. Make it easy to use on a phone, and write it the way we talk: [a few lines about us]

Built with Claude / 15 minutes to start yours / ▶︎ Watch us build it

Mary's voice guide

A cheat sheet on how Mary writes, so drafts sound like her instead of a robot. It knows she says "thank you so big" and never uses a long dash

How we built it

  1. We skipped describing her style and gave AI the real thing: emails and texts she already sent
  2. AI pulled out the patterns, like her favorite phrases, how she signs off and what she never says
  3. When a draft sounds off, she fixes it and the fix goes back into the guide

Your turn. Copy this into any AI app

Interview me one question at a time about how I write: who I usually write to, words I use a lot, and things I'd never say. After eight questions, write a short voice guide I can paste into my custom instructions.

An AI coach for a trail race

We signed up for El Cruce, a three-day trail race in Patagonia, and set up an AI coach plus a logistics helper. Then we had to coach the coach

How we built it

  1. We made one project so everything about the race lives in the same place
  2. We told it exactly what we want from a coach before asking for a single workout
  3. When it got things wrong, we said so

Your turn. Copy this into any AI app

You're my coach for [goal]. Start by asking me five questions about where I am now and what gets in my way. Then give me a plan for this week. Every time I come back, ask how it went before you give advice. Be honest, not a cheerleader.

A project for class / one episode / ▶︎ Watch how we use it

A study buddy that grills you

Before a class presentation, Mary gives the talk out loud and has AI ask the hard questions. Her review: "It grilled me more than our classmates"

How we built it

  1. One project per class with the syllabus, the readings and what Mary wants to get out of it
  2. A chat scheduled for 9pm the night before that lists what's due and what to read. For the readings she really wants to get, Mary makes a long podcast in Gemini Notebook and listens before class
  3. Before a presentation or a cold call: voice mode and two words, grill me

Your turn. Copy this into any AI app

I'm about to present [topic] for [class]. I'll give the presentation out loud first. Then act like the toughest person in the room: ask me hard questions one at a time, push back when my answer is weak and tell me where you got lost. Finish with the questions I'm most likely to get

Rules learned the hard way / one episode / ▶︎ Watch us explain them

House rules for an AI assistant

Josh's assistant keeps a list of about 80 rules. Some he gave it, some it picked up from his corrections. These are the ones that matter most

How we built it

  1. If it can be undone, it just does it. If it can't, like sending an email, it writes the draft and Josh hits send
  2. Every number needs a source. That one came from a grant application with an out-of-date number in it
  3. Before it says it can't do something, it has to try another way. It once swore Gmail was disconnected for two nights. It wasn't

Your turn. Paste this into your assistant's instructions

My house rules. If something can be undone, just do it. If it can't, like sending, buying, posting or accepting an invite, draft it and wait for my OK. Every number you give me needs a source. Before you tell me you can't do something, try another way first. When I correct you, ask if I want it saved as a new rule. Once a week, look back at our chats and suggest rules I should add
