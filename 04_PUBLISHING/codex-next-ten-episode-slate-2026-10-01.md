# Proposed next ten OOOD episodes

Prepared 2026-10-01 in response to Josh asking what the next ten episodes should be.

Status: editorial recommendation for host review. All titles, numbers and order below are provisional. No recording, schedule, results or host approval is implied. This is a slate, not ten commissioned production briefs. When selected for production, each episode should receive its own folder and template-based brief.

## Editorial recommendation

The launch establishes who the hosts are, how the AI landscape fits together, and how context, connections and reusable instructions help. The next stretch should make those ideas tangible through actual choices and results. Each episode needs a recognizable question, both hosts' independent judgment, an honest limitation, and one useful action.

Use the proposed order flexibly. The working current-developments/deep-dive cadence remains open. Comparison, research, agents, work and learning can supply five current-developments slots when a consequential release or study provides a fresh question. Writing, date planning, privacy, building and the week-long audit supply five evergreen slots. These are editorial lanes, not evidence that five news pegs already exist. Select and verify the peg near recording; do not manufacture urgency around older research. No publication dates are assigned.

## 003. We gave three AIs the same job. Which would we hire?

- Promise: choose an AI for your actual tasks rather than a universal ranking.
- Experiment: compare ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini on the same short writing task, source-based question and planning problem. Each host scores anonymized outputs before the reveal. Both defend the output they would actually use.
- Cue sheet: predict favorites; explain tasks and scoring; reveal outputs; compare preferences; check factual errors and revision effort; pick a tool for each task.
- Takeaway: run a small comparison on your own recurring work.
- Prep: record actual models, plans, settings, tools, date, inputs, cost and turnaround. Give equal context; separate factual accuracy from personal taste. No claim that this small test identifies the best AI overall. Medium preparation.

## 004. Can AI write like us without making us sound unbearable?

- Promise: improve AI-assisted writing while preserving your own judgment and voice.
- Experiment: each host supplies an approved writing sample and the same mundane communication task. Compare a generic draft, an example-guided draft and the host's final edit. Each guesses which version sounds most like the other.
- Cue sheet: read one actual awkward line; state the communication goal; compare drafts aloud; explain which edits matter; show a failure; offer a reusable starting instruction.
- Takeaway: give AI an example of your writing and a clear audience, then edit for meaning.
- Prep: use material cleared for public use; do not invent the awkward result or promise perfect imitation. This extends E002 through an outcome instead of redefining skills. Low preparation.

## 005. AI did the research. We checked the receipts.

- Promise: distinguish a persuasive answer from a supported one.
- Experiment: research one real, low-stakes decision and audit the citations. One host traces the source; the other checks whether it supports the exact claim, then swap roles. Keep successful sourcing as well as any mistakes.
- Cue sheet: choose the decision; predict what would convince you; examine the report; follow sources; separate facts and inference; decide what remains unknown.
- Takeaway: open the source behind the claim that would change your decision.
- Prep: choose a bounded question; preserve full queries and outputs; verify source dates and scope. Do not promise hallucinations or generalize from one report. The AI Fluency Index supplies background on evaluation behaviors, not a factual-error rate. [Source pack](../02_RESEARCH/codex-next-ten-episode-sources-2026-10-01.md). Medium preparation.

## 006. Can AI plan a date we'd actually go on?

- Promise: show how preferences and real constraints change a planning task.
- Experiment: each host independently gives AI a shared budget and basic constraints plus their own preferences. Reveal two plans, choose one together and try it before recording the verdict if practical.
- Cue sheet: define a good date independently; predict suggestions; reveal plans; check feasibility; share the actual outing if completed; explain the one instruction that helped.
- Takeaway: describe what a good experience feels like, not just its category.
- Prep: verify hours, prices, travel and availability before use. Outcomes remain unknown; an outing is optional and must be described as proposed unless completed. Do not force disagreement. Medium preparation including any outing.

## 007. Would you tell AI that?

- Promise: make informed choices about what to share with an AI service.
- Experiment: each host sorts fictional scenarios independently: a shopping list, personal journal, internal work plan and someone else's information. Compare reasoning, then inspect relevant product settings and policies.
- Cue sheet: reveal independent choices; discuss why boundaries differ; distinguish memory, training, retention and external-service access; check current account settings; revise a choice if warranted.
- Takeaway: check the controls for your account before providing information you care about.
- Prep: use synthetic examples, not private participant/donor records. Provider and account differences matter. OpenAI's documentation supports distinguishing training controls from memory and retention; it is not a guarantee of confidentiality or a complete multi-provider comparison. [Source pack](../02_RESEARCH/codex-next-ten-episode-sources-2026-10-01.md). Medium preparation.

## 008. We gave an AI agent a to-do list. How much babysitting did it need?

- Promise: understand when an AI that takes actions is useful and how much supervision the task requires.
- Experiment: choose a bounded public-information task that produces a local result, such as researching event options and preparing a comparison. Define success before the run. Count corrections, checks, retries and review time.
- Cue sheet: explain the job and expected result; both predict failure points; inspect the actual run; distinguish suggested action from completed action; evaluate net effort; choose what you would delegate next.
- Takeaway: give a clear completion condition and decide where human review belongs.
- Prep: verify actual tool capabilities and permissions. Publishing/sending is not needed for this experiment. Anthropic's workflow/agent distinction is conceptual background; its older tooling examples are not a current capability map. [Source pack](../02_RESEARCH/codex-next-ten-episode-sources-2026-10-01.md). Higher preparation.

## 009. Can we build an app we'd actually use?

- Promise: show what it takes to turn a small idea into something usable with AI assistance.
- Experiment: each host names a real annoyance; pick one small solution together. Possible prompts include a shared decision helper or event checklist. Build a prototype before recording and have both hosts try to break it.
- Cue sheet: explain the annoyance; agree on must-have behavior; show the first result; test the real task; expose a bug or limitation if one occurs; decide whether a simpler tool would work better.
- Takeaway: define a tiny useful job and test it before adding features.
- Prep: preserve actual build time, costs, fixes and failures. Use synthetic data. A working prototype is not evidence of production readiness, security or maintainability. Avoid calling either host a non-coder without confirming that framing. Higher preparation; do not make the episode depend on a live build succeeding.

## 010. Could AI do our jobs?

- Promise: separate tasks AI can assist with from the judgment and relationships involved in running an organization.
- Experiment: map an approved slice of Run Your City work, then test one bounded task. Possible areas: an event plan, a partner brief or an internal comparison. Mary assesses founder/CEO priorities; Josh assesses operating effort. Both challenge the proposed delegation.
- Cue sheet: describe the real job; list its component tasks; predict what transfers well; test one; evaluate review and relationship costs; name what stays with the people doing the work.
- Takeaway: start with a task map before deciding what to automate.
- Prep: hosts choose the public examples. Do not imply measured savings, sector-wide adoption rates, workforce forecasts or replacement of either host. Generalize the task-selection method so lean teams beyond nonprofits can use it. Medium preparation.

## 011. Is AI helping us learn, or helping us avoid learning?

- Promise: distinguish getting an answer from gaining the ability to explain or use it.
- Experiment: each host learns a comparable unfamiliar concept with AI, using an answer-oriented or tutor-oriented approach, then explains it without the tool. Reverse approaches on another concept if feasible. Discuss the method alongside the actual outcome.
- Cue sheet: predict what you will remember; define approaches; share learning experiences; attempt an unaided explanation; discuss the study's narrow scope; choose one learning habit.
- Takeaway: ask AI to question you and test yourself after closing the chat.
- Prep: use a non-graded exercise. The host comparison is illustrative, not a controlled trial. Shen and Tamkin's coding experiment concerns unfamiliar programming tasks, not proof that AI makes everyone less intelligent. [Source pack](../02_RESEARCH/codex-next-ten-episode-sources-2026-10-01.md). Medium preparation.

## 012. We used AI for a week. What was actually worth it?

- Promise: distinguish an impressive demonstration from a habit worth keeping.
- Experiment: start a shared log while testing earlier ideas. Record setup, use, checks, fixes, costs and whether you returned to the workflow. Each host chooses what to keep and abandon independently before discussing.
- Cue sheet: predict keepers; reveal independently selected favorites; inspect the work log; include a miss; compare total effort and output quality; choose next week's one habit.
- Takeaway: audit one recurring workflow including the time spent correcting and reviewing.
- Prep: start logging early. Estimate avoided effort explicitly rather than presenting it as measured. Show personal outcomes without promising universal savings. The experiment must happen before using the working title as a factual publication title. Medium preparation spread over a week.

## Why someone watches these two

The comparison, writing and date episodes let Josh and Mary reveal independent preferences. The agent and build episodes create shared stakes around an actual result. The work episode gives both hosts substantive professional judgment, and the learning/audit episodes create room to reconsider earlier expectations. None requires a predetermined disagreement, joke or failure.

Keep spoken explanations complete for audio listeners. Screen captures can supply supporting evidence; a permanent screen-share format remains a host decision. Keep scope small enough to describe inputs, show results and evaluate them honestly.
