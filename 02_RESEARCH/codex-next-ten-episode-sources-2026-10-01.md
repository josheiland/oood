## Evaluating polished outputs: episodes 003, 005, 009 and 012

- Source: Anthropic Education Report: The AI Fluency Index.
- Publisher: Anthropic / Claude Academy.
- URL: https://academy.claude.com/tutorials/the-ai-fluency-index
- Publication date: page says originally published 2026-02-23; its embedded citation carries 2026-02-16. Preserve that discrepancy rather than inventing a single authoritative date.
- Supported claim: in the studied multi-turn Claude conversations, iteration correlates with other observable fluency behaviors; conversations producing artifacts show lower rates of some explicit evaluation behaviors.
- Method/scope: 9,830 conversations during one week in January 2026; 11 observable indicators. Observational, provider-produced research, not a randomized intervention or cross-provider comparison.
- Limits: cannot see evaluation outside the chat; correlations do not establish causality, polished-output errors or users' underlying competence. Do not use it to claim that all users blindly trust AI.
- Slate use: motivates checking an actual output and its sources, not an asserted outcome or statistic in a script.

## Agent versus predefined workflow: episode 008

- Source: Building effective agents.
- Publisher: Anthropic Engineering.
- URL: https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents
- Publication date: 2024-12-19.
- Supported claim: Anthropic distinguishes predefined workflows from agents that dynamically direct process/tool use; it describes evaluation, environmental feedback and cost/error tradeoffs.
- Limits: provider-authored engineering guidance, not independent evidence of labor savings. The page explicitly notes that tooling has changed since publication. Use only the conceptual distinction; inspect current tools before choosing the demonstration.
- Slate use: define the task's completion condition and evaluate supervision effort.

## Data settings are separate concepts: episode 007

- Source: Data controls in ChatGPT.
- Publisher: OpenAI Help Center.
- URL: https://help.openai.com/en/articles/7730893-data-controls-in-chatgpt
- Publication date: undated live help page; relative update label observed, no exact calendar publication date established.
- Supported claim: available controls depend on plan/account/workspace; turning off model improvement excludes new conversations from training but does not remove them from history. Memory/personalization, training and retention are distinct. Temporary chats can be retained for safety purposes.
- Limits: official product policy, not a third-party audit or a blanket confidentiality guarantee. Other providers and external-service permissions require separate checks. Inspect the hosts' actual accounts before recommending specific settings; no settings were changed for this research.
- Slate use: compare fictional information-sharing scenarios and check policies without exposing private organizational records.

## Task completion versus learning: episode 011

- Source: How AI Impacts Skill Formation, Judy Hanwen Shen and Alex Tamkin.
- Publisher: authors via arXiv; Anthropic-associated research.
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20245
- Publication date: submitted 2026-01-28; v2 revised 2026-02-01.
- Supported claim: the authors report randomized experiments learning an unfamiliar asynchronous programming library; AI assistance reduced measured conceptual understanding, code reading and debugging in that setting, without significant average efficiency gains. They also describe interaction patterns involving cognitive engagement that preserve learning outcomes.
- Limits: preprint and narrow programming setting; abstract checked for this recommendation. Do not infer general cognitive decline, universal effects across subjects or long-term learning outcomes. Review the complete paper and study design before selecting on-air numbers. The two-host learning exercise is illustrative, not a replication.
- Slate use: justify a question about learning method rather than a predetermined claim that AI makes users smarter or less capable.
