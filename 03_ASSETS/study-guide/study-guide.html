Why we made this

We're Mary and Josh. We started Out of Our Depth because AI was moving faster than we could keep up, and we were tired of nodding along to words we only half understood

This is the guide we wish someone had handed us. Words in plain English, habits that keep you out of trouble, and fun things to build. You can read it in an afternoon (if you're not dyslexic, sry Mary)

Generative AI

AI that makes new things, like writing, images, music or video

Think of it like The difference between a spam filter and a ghostwriter

Prompt

Whatever you type or say to the AI. The best ones read like a text to a smart friend who knows nothing about your life

Think of it like Ordering "food, please" vs. "the salmon, sauce on the side."

Large language model

The engine inside ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini. It read a huge chunk of the internet and learned to guess the next word

Model vs. app

Who's who

OpenAI is the company. GPT is its family of models. ChatGPT is the app you open. Same idea for Anthropic, Claude models and the Claude app

Think of it like Toyota, the engine and the Prius

Hallucination

When AI makes something up with a straight face, like a quote nobody said or a study that doesn't exist

Think of it like The friend who'd rather invent an answer than say "I don't know."

▶︎ Watch ep 003 at 22:09

Multimodal

AI that understands more than text. Send it a photo or a voice memo

Think of it like Snap a photo of your fridge and ask what's for dinner

Jagged frontier

AI is amazing at some things and weirdly bad at others, and the split isn't what you'd guess. The only way to find the edges is to try it on your own stuff

Think of it like A genius who still can't fold a fitted sheet

Chatbot vs. assistant

A chatbot talks. An assistant talks and does things for you, like searching, reading your files or booking the table. Most AI apps started as the first and are turning into the second

Think of it like A pen pal vs. a coworker

Voice mode

Talking to AI out loud and hearing it talk back. Great for walks, dishes and thinking out loud

Think of it like A phone call instead of a text thread

Image generation

AI that makes pictures from a description. It builds a brand new image instead of finding an old one

Think of it like A sketch artist who draws whatever you describe

Make it yours

Stop re-explaining yourself every chat. Set it up once and it starts smarter every time

Memory

The app remembering things about you between chats, like your job or that you're vegetarian. You can edit it in settings

Think of it like A barista who knows your usual

Custom instructions

Notes the AI reads before every chat, like "keep it short" or "I run a nonprofit."

Think of it like The note on the fridge for the babysitter

▶︎ Watch ep 010 at 19:22

Projects

A folder for one ongoing thing, with its own chats, files and instructions, so you never re-explain the backstory

Think of it like Giving your AI its own desk

▶︎ Watch ep 001 at 01:43

Model sizes

Most AI companies sell a few sizes of the same model. Bigger is smarter, smaller is faster and cheaper, and the names change every few months

Think of it like Coffee sizes. Not every question needs a venti

Reasoning model

Thinking mode

A model that thinks through a problem before answering. Slower, but much better at math and anything with moving parts

Think of it like Showing your work instead of blurting out the first number

Deep research

Ask a big question, go make coffee, come back to a report with sources. The AI runs dozens of searches for you

Think of it like An intern who does the library run for you

System prompt

The hidden instructions an app gives the AI before you type a word. It's why the same model acts differently in different apps

Think of it like The staff handbook you never get to read

Level 0.4 / 6 ft / 11 words

Spot the traps

AI fails quietly and confidently. These are the ways it happens, and the easy habits that protect you

Your data

Privacy settings

On free and personal plans, your chats can be used to train future models unless you switch that off in settings. Either way, don't paste in passwords, account numbers or anything you'd hate to see again

Think of it like Talking in a coffee shop. Mostly fine, but you lower your voice for some things

▶︎ Watch ep 002 at 37:58

Knowledge cutoff

The date a model's built-in knowledge stops. Ask about anything newer and it's guessing, unless it can search the web

Think of it like A smart friend back from six months off the grid

AI slop

The bland, over-polished writing AI churns out when nobody edits it. "I hope this finds you well," forever

Think of it like Elevator music, but for words

▶︎ Watch ep 001 at 15:42

Sycophancy

When AI tells you what you want to hear, like calling your so-so business plan brilliant

Think of it like A yes-man with a big vocabulary

▶︎ Watch ep 004 at 23:43

Bias

AI learned from human writing, so it picked up our blind spots too, like picturing every CEO as a man. Notice it, and ask for other angles

Think of it like A mirror that can also magnify

Deepfake

Fake but real-looking video, audio or photos of a real person. Voice clones make scam calls sound like family

Think of it like Photoshop, if Photoshop could call your grandma

Prompt injection

Sneaky instructions hidden in a webpage or email that trick AI into doing something you didn't ask for

Think of it like A note slipped into the to-do pile: "also, wire $500 to this account."

Jailbreak

Tricking an AI into breaking its own rules, usually with a sneaky story or a role-play

Think of it like Convincing the bouncer you're on the list

AI companion

An AI built to feel like a friend or a partner. It can be comforting, and it's also designed to keep you coming back

Think of it like A friend whose paycheck depends on your screen time

Watermark

A hidden marker that says AI made this. Helpful when it's there, but easy to strip out, so no watermark doesn't mean it's real

Think of it like A "made in" tag that can be cut off

AI detector

A tool that guesses whether a human or an AI wrote something. They get it wrong a lot, in both directions, so never treat one as proof

Think of it like A lie detector that's also guessing

Come up for air / name that moment

  1. It called your business plan genius. Again
  2. It has no idea who won last night's game
  3. A celebrity is selling a miracle cream they've never heard of

1 Sycophancy / 2 Knowledge cutoff / 3 Deepfake

Dare Ask it to roast your favorite idea instead of praising it

Under the hood

Just enough of how it works to follow the news, ask good questions and not get fooled by hype

Training

How a model learns. It reads a mountain of text over and over until the patterns stick

Think of it like Years of reading before the first day on the job

▶︎ Watch ep 002 at 06:20

Token

The bite-size chunks AI reads and writes in, about three quarters of a word each. Prices and limits are counted in them

Think of it like Gas in the car. Every word in and out burns a little

▶︎ Watch ep 002 at 13:26

Context window

How much the AI can hold in its head at once, including your messages, its replies and your files. In long chats, the early stuff starts to slip

Think of it like A whiteboard. Fill it up and something gets erased

▶︎ Watch ep 001 at 03:12

Open vs. closed models

Open models, like DeepSeek and Qwen, can be downloaded and run by anyone. Closed ones, like the models in ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, can't be downloaded. You use them through the company's own apps, or through other apps that plug into them

Think of it like A recipe you can cook at home vs. a dish you can only order at the restaurant

▶︎ Watch ep 002 at 06:20

Benchmark

A standardized test for AI. Labs love bragging about scores, and the tests keep getting harder because models keep acing them

Think of it like The SAT, for robots

▶︎ Watch ep 008 at 14:53

GPU

Graphics processing unit

The chips that do AI's heavy math. Nvidia makes most of them, and every AI company wants more

Think of it like Gaming graphics cards that got drafted into a much bigger job

▶︎ Watch ep 013 at 07:36

Data center

Warehouses full of those chips, running around the clock. Their power and water use is a big part of the AI debate

Think of it like The kitchen behind every AI answer

▶︎ Watch ep 012 at 01:05

RAG

Retrieval-augmented generation

AI that checks your documents before answering, so it can cite sources instead of going from memory

Think of it like An open-book test instead of a closed-book one

Fine-tuning

Extra training on a focused set of examples so a model gets great at one job, like writing in your company's voice

Think of it like A residency after med school

▶︎ Watch ep 002 at 11:24

API

The side door that lets other apps plug into an AI model. It's how AI shows up inside tools that aren't chat apps

Think of it like The drive-through window. Apps order without ever walking in

Inference

The AI actually answering you. Training is the studying. Inference is taking the test, every time you hit send

Think of it like Cooking the meal, not writing the cookbook

Compute

The raw computing power AI runs on. When companies say they need more of it, they mean more chips, more data centers and more electricity

Think of it like Horsepower, for AI

Come up for air / name that moment

  1. Long chat, and it forgot what you said at the start
  2. A new model "crushes every test" and you still can't tell if it's good
  3. The company says it needs more chips, more power, more everything

1 Context window / 2 Benchmark / 3 Compute

Dare Ask it how many tokens are in your full name

AI that does things

The jump from answering questions to actually getting stuff done for you, with you still in charge

Agent

AI you give a goal instead of a question. It picks the steps, uses tools like search or your calendar, and keeps going until it's done

Think of it like Asking for directions vs. handing someone your car keys

▶︎ Watch all of ep 003

Skills

Saved instructions for specific jobs, like your brand rules or how you write a grant. The AI grabs the right one when it needs it

Think of it like A binder of how-tos a new hire actually reads

▶︎ Watch ep 001 at 08:12

MCP

Model Context Protocol

A standard plug that connects AI to your other apps, like Gmail or Google Drive, so it can get things done there

Think of it like USB for AI. One plug shape, lots of devices

▶︎ Watch ep 001 at 05:59

Computer use

AI using a computer the way you do, looking at the screen, clicking and typing

Think of it like Handing someone your laptop and watching over their shoulder

Vibe coding

Building an app by describing it in plain English and letting AI write all the code

Think of it like Telling a builder what house you want and never looking at the blueprints

▶︎ Watch ep 002 at 21:23

Human in the loop

You stay the one who checks and signs off, especially before AI sends, buys or posts anything for you. Your name is on it, not the AI's

Think of it like Autopilot still needs a pilot

Come up for air / name that moment

  1. You asked it to plan the trip. It planned it and booked it
  2. You described an app in plain English and got a working app
  3. It drafts the email, but you hit send

The big questions

The debates everyone is having. Nobody has the answers yet, so your take counts

Scaling laws

The pattern that models get smarter as they get bigger and train on more data with more chips. Whether it keeps holding is the trillion-dollar question

Think of it like Adding horsepower to a car. Works great, until it doesn't

Alignment

Getting AI to do what people actually want, without cutting corners or causing harm. It matters more as models get smarter

Think of it like Raising a kid who'll end up smarter than you

AGI

Artificial general intelligence

AI that can do pretty much any thinking job a person can. Nobody agrees on the definition or when it arrives

Think of it like A finish line everyone's racing toward, but each team drew it somewhere else

Superintelligence

AI that beats the best humans at nearly everything. The step after AGI

Think of it like The student who passes the teacher and keeps going

The whole thing on one page

Learn, look, think, act

Almost every AI headline is about one of these four steps. Figure out which one, and the news gets a lot less confusing

Learn

Training. The model reads a mountain of text and the patterns stick. Happens months before you ever open the app

Training, GPUs, data centers, fine-tuning
Look

Pulling in fresh info the moment you ask: a web search, your files, your inbox

RAG, deep research, MCP
Think

Working out the answer, one token at a time, inside the context window

Tokens, context window, inference
Act

Doing something in the world: sending, booking, clicking, writing code

Agents, skills, computer use

The bottom / no right answers

10 debates

We don't agree on half of these. Pick a side, then go start something at dinner

1. Would you be mad if AI wrote your apology?

A Yes. The effort is the apology

B No. The right words are the right words

2. Should kids use AI for homework?

A Yes. It's a tutor that never gets tired

B No. The struggle is the learning

3. Is AI making us better or just faster?

A Better. It handles the busywork so we can think

B Faster. We're skipping the part where we learn

4. If an AI makes you feel understood, does it matter that nobody understood you?

A It matters. Feeling heard needs a someone

B It doesn't. Comfort is comfort

5. Do you have to tell people when AI helped?

A Always. Hiding it is a little bit lying

B Nope. Nobody discloses spellcheck

6. Is it still yours if the idea was yours and the words weren't?

A Yes. The idea is the hard part

B No. The words are the work

7. Will AI take your job or just the boring parts?

A The boring parts. The job gets better

B The job. The boring parts were the job

8. Who should make the rules for AI?

A The people building it. They understand it

B Everyone else. They have to live with it

9. Would you hand an AI your credit card?

A Sure, with a spending limit

B Not until it can explain itself

10. Smarter-than-human AI: exciting or terrifying?

A Exciting. Think cures and free time

B Terrifying. Nobody knows how to steer it

The parts that need us

What we still do ourselves

  • The ideas and the opinions. AI can argue both sides, but our take is ours
  • The jokes, and the real replies to real people
  • The final say on anything with our names on it
  • Anything that's supposed to sound like it came from our hearts

Steal our homework

5 fun ways to get started

The fastest way to learn AI is to make something with it. Pick one, paste the prompt into any AI app, and fill in the [brackets]

5 min / Any app with photos

What's for dinner?

Take a photo of your fridge. AI looks at the picture and plans dinner around what's in there

You get Three dinners you can make tonight, plus a tiny shopping list

Copy this into any AI app

Here's a photo of my fridge. Give me three dinners I can make tonight with what's in here, plus anything I'd need to grab. Keep each one under 30 minutes

15 min / Custom instructions / ▶︎ ep 005

Teach it your voice

AI interviews you about how you write, then writes a cheat sheet on your style. Paste it in once and drafts stop sounding like a robot

You get A one-page voice guide you can reuse everywhere

What we made Mary's guide was built from her real emails and texts. It knows she says "thank you so big," signs off "Fired up," and never uses a long dash

Copy this into any AI app

Interview me one question at a time about how I write: who I usually write to, words I use a lot, and things I'd never say. After eight questions, write a short voice guide I can paste into my custom instructions

15 min / Projects / ▶︎ ep 004

Your own coach

Set up one chat that knows your goal and asks how it went every time you come back

You get A plan for this week and a coach that remembers last week

What we made We built a coach and a logistics helper for our El Cruce trail race, then had to coach the coach. It's all in ep 004

Copy this into any AI app

You're my coach for [goal]. Start by asking me five questions about where I am now and what gets in my way. Then give me a plan for this week. Every time I come back, ask how it went before you give advice. Be honest, not a cheerleader

10 min / Vibe coding

Build a tiny app

Describe an app in plain English and AI writes the code. You never have to read any of it

You get A working bill splitter you can open on your phone

What we made This whole website. We described it and Claude built it

Copy this into any AI app

Make me a simple, colorful web app that splits a dinner bill. I type the total, the tip and everyone's names, and it shows what each person owes. Make it easy to use on my phone

10 min / Thinking partner

Settle a debate

Hand AI both sides of an argument and ask for the best case for each. Think referee, not judge

You get The strongest version of each side, and what would change each mind

Copy this into any AI app

My partner and I disagree about [topic]. Give the strongest case for each side, then tell us what evidence would change each of our minds. Don't pick a winner

Happens to everyone

If you get stuck

It sounds generic Show it, don't describe it. Paste an example you love and say "more like this."

It got something wrong Tell it exactly what's off, like you'd tell a new coworker. Then ask it to double check its own answer

It agrees with everything Ask "What would a smart skeptic say?" or "What am I missing?"

It forgot what you said Long chats slip. Start a fresh chat with a quick summary, or keep the work in a project

You don't know what to ask Let it interview you: "Here's my week. What could you help with?"
